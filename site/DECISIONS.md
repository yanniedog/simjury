# Web player decisions (simjury.com)

The active and only player is **SimJury Court Week** at `/`. The full owner
decision is [`../COURT-WEEK.md`](../COURT-WEEK.md). Legacy `/today`, `/play` and
`/install` paths and the former `/jury/` route redirect to `/`.

## D-WEB-1 — Audio-first, reviewed media

- “Take your seat” is the single user gesture that primes audio and may request
  native fullscreen. CSS immersion remains complete when either is unavailable.
- Dialogue is pre-generated by trusted manual GitHub Actions and published as
  content-addressed assets under the exact Release tag pinned by the manifest.
  Never fetch `latest` and never synthesize speech on a server during play.
- Scene audio is resumable. Device speech and then reading mode are fallbacks;
  a media error may not advance or corrupt legal state.
- GitHub may observe media request metadata, timing and opaque identifiers. It
  receives no local progress, private notes or ballots from SimJury.

## D-WEB-2 — Court Week deliberation

- The player deliberates with eleven authored jurors. There are no live people,
  accounts, chat service or runtime-generated replies.
- The player seals a provisional position before an anonymous aggregate ballot
  appears. Individual positions remain sealed throughout.
- Every contribution starts with a legal element or verdict question, admitted
  evidence and a reasoning technique. Free text is optional and stays private.
- Reliance on punishment, silence, character or struck evidence is corrected and
  can reduce the contribution's authored influence.

## D-WEB-3 — Responsive courtroom art

- Court-sketch/cinematic tableaux use phone portrait, tablet 4:3 and desktop
  16:9 compositions. `<picture>`, `srcset` and `sizes` select a rendition; one
  image is never blindly cropped across devices.
- Every scene declares focal, subject-safe, evidence-safe and caption-safe
  regions. Legally material detail is available in accessible text and in the
  opaque evidence viewer at equivalent resolution on every device.
- Artwork contains no real likeness and no baked-in readable dialogue.

## D-WEB-4 — Fiction and adult entry

- SimJury identifies the whole experience as fictional and for adults aged 18+
  in one combined entry acknowledgement remembered in the device-local profile.
  The case does not repeat that disclaimer during play.
- No real person, organisation, brand, event or specific real location appears
  in player-visible content. Serious crime is direct but non-graphic and omits
  reusable instructions for harm.

## D-WEB-5 — Static-only hosting and local state

- Cloudflare serves Static Assets only. No Worker, API, D1, Durable Object,
  WebSocket, waitlist, Discord handler, server-side analytics or runtime AI is
  allowed. The reviewed client-side Microsoft Clarity tag is the sole analytics
  exception: source is fully masked, advertising storage is denied, automated
  sessions are tagged and a browser-local opt-out prevents future tag loading.
- Progress, private notes and ballots live in IndexedDB. The small local profile
  (juror label, adult-fiction acknowledgement and default-off Developer mode)
  lives in validated localStorage. It is not an account and is never transmitted.
  A versioned local export/import file is the only cross-device transfer mechanism.
- Old Daily Docket local-storage records remain untouched but are never read.

## D-WEB-6 — Device parity

- The supported browser matrix and minimum/maximum viewports in `COURT-WEEK.md`
  are release gates, not best-effort targets.
- No orientation lock, hover-only action, drag-only control or native-fullscreen
  dependency. Touch targets are at least 44px and 200% zoom must not introduce
  horizontal scrolling.
- Captions are optional in audio-first mode and mandatory when audio fails. No
  legal fact may be exclusive to image, crop, colour or sound.

## Removed decisions

Daily case supply, a seven-case selector, guided introduction, streaks, live
jury rooms, Discord and the email waitlist are retired. Their documentation and
data-retirement record remain provenance, not product authority.
